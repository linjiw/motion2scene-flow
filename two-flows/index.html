Two Flows

Learn the scenes that make a humanoid's motion necessary, then learn the motion those scenes demand.

motion → sceneSceneFlow generates certified scene–motion pairs from motion data alone
scene → motionFlow planner plans whole-body motion; the SONIC tracker executes it
↻ cycleCycleFlow the planner's likelihood scores the generator's scenes

The problem

Motion data has no scenes. Scene data has no motions.

A planner learns only what its data makes necessary

If every training scene can be walked around, the planner learns to walk around. To duck, crouch, squeeze or turn sideways it needs pairs where that motion is the only one that fits.

Mocap is rich but scene-less

Kimodo-G1 generations and Bones-SEED captures (142k clips, 288 h) show how people duck, side-step and stoop, with no record of what they avoided.

Hand-written rules are narrow

Our first data engine placed one rule obstacle per motion type. It gave the one big data win (+15.3 pp held out, F23), but its style leaked into our test panels (F60, F83).

Our approach

  1. Learn the scene from the motion (SceneFlow) and keep only pairs an exact verifier certifies as necessary.
  2. Learn the motion from the scene (a whole-body flow planner), executed by the frozen SONIC tracker in physics.
  3. Close the loop (CycleFlow): keep the scenes the planner reads as explaining the motion.
  4. Judge on scenes no generator built: the certified G1 Traverse Arena, sealed test splits.

Flow 1 · SceneFlow · the target

A scene is useful when the motion fits it and its alternatives do not

Held-out Kimodo duck. Grey: the motion passes with 5.5 cm to spare. Red: the same path walked upright hits the beam.

Three exact checks per scene

feasible
the motion clears every box by ≥ 4 cm (45 body capsules, hands included)
necessary
the walking alternative from the same past collides
right cause
a duck is explained by height: upright on the same path must collide. A detour is explained by position: its posture on the straight path must collide
Alternatives come from real sibling futures (Kimodo continuations of one prefix, 15 SONIC skills) or, for raw mocap, from the motion's own gait carried straight ahead. No per-behaviour rules.
Without the cause check a duck that drifts 20 cm sideways gets "explained" by a pillar. That was the 0% cause-recovery failure of the earlier LfLH-style generator.

Flow 1 · SceneFlow · model

A conditional set flow from body points to boxes

Training

Rectified flow, xt = (1−t)ε + t·x1, target x1 − ε, on engine-certified pairs (the data engine grows boxes from critical points of the alternatives, then verifies them). Mirror augmentation. v3c: Kimodo contrast + 4× Bones decision points + posture-rich Kimodo, posture scenes ×3.

Two-sided guidance

From t = 0.5 the clean estimate is nudged so the motion keeps its 5 cm margin and the motion's own straight gait overlaps a box. Needs nothing but the motion, so it works on raw mocap.

Tunable

"3 obstacles, overhead only, 8 cm margin, necessary" is one call: each knob has a learned null, so any subset can be set and the rest marginalised (classifier-free guidance over knobs).

Flow 2 · planner · model

A whole-body flow planner that the tracker can follow

Teacher (privileged map)

The current default teacher C is trained only on cycle-filtered SceneFlow scenes over 148 contrast groups (Kimodo and SONIC continuations; 15,264 windows, 3 seeds).

Lidar student

The same network on maps built from a simulated head MID-360 (ray-cast memory, its own route). On fresh layouts it matched its privileged teacher, 207 vs 208 of 252 (F72).

What search adds

Guidance pushes candidates out of the 8 cm execution envelope while they are being sampled; selection then needs no luck. The network proposes, search decides.

How the planner learns · 1 · from pairs to training examples

One SceneFlow pair becomes many training windows

Teacher C's real training data (Kimodo group g00001, duck future, its cycle-filtered SceneFlow scene). Every 0.1 s an anchor cuts a window; the inset is the map the network gets for that window.

One training example

history
4 frames (80 ms) of the measured 65-D state, ending at the anchor
target Y
the next 25 frames (0.5 s at 50 Hz) of the real motion
goal
where the motion is 1 s later: x, y, cos Δyaw, sin Δyaw in the anchor frame
map
the ≤ 8 nearest boxes, in the anchor frame, rasterized into a 3 × 31 × 61 map (5 cm cells, 3 m × 1.5 m around the torso)
anchors
every 0.1 s from 0.5 s before to 1.2 s after the branch: up to 18 windows per pair

The 65-D state, per frame

root height1gravity in the root frame3
planar velocity (heading frame)2yaw rate1
29 joint angles2929 joint velocities29

Global position and yaw are not predicted: they are integrated from the anchor. Height and joints are learned relative to the anchor frame (residual), then normalized.

How the planner learns · 2 · why the pairs teach the map

Same past, different scene, different future

One group, nine futures. The walk keeps an empty scene; every other future gets the SceneFlow scene that makes it necessary. Up to the branch the nine windows are the same (to within 1 cm).
With the same history and nearly the same goal, the map is the only input that tells the duck from the walk. Minimizing the loss on these windows forces the network to read it. Without such pairs, a planner can predict the future from the history alone and ignore the map (our September diagnostics: identical outputs for empty, true and irrelevant scenes).

What one batch of 128 holds

83,444 training windows: 68,180 from the Kimodo indoor-navigation corpus (walking and turning, with empty or compatible scenes) and 15,264 SceneFlow necessity windows from 148 contrast groups, repeated ×4 so they fill ~47% of every batch.

How the planner learns · 3 · the network

A transformer that reads the map by cross-attention

Why history as tokens

The current state enters the attention directly. Earlier versions conditioned only through a pooled vector and drifted; the residual frame and history tokens cut the first-frame jump from 0.78 to 0.056 rad (21 Sept.).

Why cross-attention to the map

Each future frame can look up the cells it will pass through. The pooled map token also modulates every block, so the whole chunk knows "there is a beam ahead".

Per-token flow time

Each token carries its own t, so a later replan can keep frames already committed and denoise the rest. Teacher C trains with one t per window (soft RTC off).

How the planner learns · 4 · the loss

Flow matching: learn the velocity that turns noise into the real future

One real training window (the duck, 0.2 s before the branch, fixed noise). Top: what the network is given. Bottom: the clean future it recovers in a single step, Ŷ = x_t − t·v. White ghost: the truth at +0.5 s.
xt = (1 − t)·Y + t·ε
t ~ U(0, 1),  ε ~ N(0, I)
target velocity u = ε − Y
ℒ = Huber( vθ(xt, t | history, goal, map) − u )
  • Y lives in the normalized, anchor-relative 65-D space; the loss averages over 25 frames × 65 numbers.
  • Data at t = 0, noise at t = 1. At t = 1 the network must guess the whole future from history, goal and map; near t = 0 the future is visible and only the noise is unknown.
  • No geometry, contact or collision terms in teacher C: scene awareness has to come from the paired data. Clearance enters later, as guidance at sampling time.

AdamW, lr 2×10⁻⁴, weight decay 0.01, gradient clip 1.0, batch 128, 30,000 steps (~30 epochs), 21 minutes on one RTX 5080.

How the planner learns · 6 · what it learned

Hide the beam, and the trained network ducks less

Same past, same goal, same 16 noises; only the map differs. The real boxes are drawn faintly on the right so you can see what the network was not shown.
0.63 mmean lowest pelvis, beam in the map
0.74 mmean lowest pelvis, empty map

The true duck goes down to 0.61 m. The map is worth about 11 cm of crouch here, read 0.2 s before the branch. On duck windows from three groups the gap is 6–11 cm just before the branch, and it fades once the history already shows the descent.

The network reads the map, but shallowly. It cannot pick the true scene out of 8 (F77), and in closed loop its raw proposals barely change with more data variety (F89). Guidance at sampling time supplies the rest. That split, learned proposals plus geometric search, is what runs on the robot, next.

At deployment

10 Euler steps from noise, two 0.5 s chunks chained (chunk 2 starts from chunk 1's last 4 frames), 16 candidates in parallel, clearance guidance, select the safe one closest to the goal, SONIC tracks it, replan every 0.16 s.

CycleFlow · closing the loop between the two flows

Necessity is information

The motion should be more likely under the planner once the generated scene is shown. With −ℓφ as a surrogate for log q(m | s), the Barber–Agakov bound makes the expected gain a lower bound on the information I(M; S) the scene carries about the motion.

  • Not CycleGAN's L1 cycle. Both maps are many-to-many: one beam admits many ducks, one duck many beams. An L1 round trip collapses that diversity (the Augmented CycleGAN result).
  • Verifiers replace discriminators. Only exactly verified scenes are scored, so there is no adversarial game.
  • Common random numbers. ℓ is the flow-matching loss under the same 16 (t, ε) draws for every scene, so differences are low-variance.
  • No prior motion ↔ scene cycle found in the literature review; closest is Li et al., SIGGRAPH 2024 (arXiv 2405.12460).

The judge · G1 Traverse Arena

A benchmark no generator built, with necessity certified per scene

  • Why. Our old panels shared a 15 cm beam primitive with the rule data, and every family had an upright detour (F60). BARN saw the same: same-generator test worlds saturate.
  • Certificate. Dijkstra over (cell × posture mode: stand, duck, crouch, squeeze, duck-squeeze) on exact clearance fields. A skill is required when no route exists without it.
  • Real objects. Doors, desks, racks, pallets, cars, gate arms, scaffold ledgers, planks on supports; dimensions from building codes and catalogs. Nothing floats.
  • Clutter that keeps the goal reachable, so density adds no rejection bias (1,044 of 1,044 slots admitted).

Tiers A/B · arena-v1

1,044 scenes, 17 families from park paths to lobby queues and warehouse aisles. Sealed test splits: held-out instances, held-out families, extrapolation.

Tier C · whole body

371 scenes (arena-c3) whose gates need arms in, side-on or ducking. Every gate is passed by a SONIC-executed witness under ±4.4 cm shifts.

Preview namespace only; galleries never touch test instances (per-scene sha256 manifest, test commitment hash).

Tiers A/B fail on proposals

Most contacts follow "no safe plan": none of the 16 candidates fits the clutter. Tight margins did not change the count (16 = 16 of 52). Motion and scene variety is the lever, so variety tests read tiers A/B.

Tier C fails on execution

Allowing squeezes turns "no plan" into "plan executed 4–7 cm off". Forcing the witnesses' arm tuck and side heading executes (arm span 75 → 47 cm), but thighs and lateral precision then limit (F80, F85).

Rules we hold ourselves to

  • Learned generator only from 9 Oct; no more rule-vs-learned panels (the old ones resemble the rule's scenes).
  • Rounds are registered with adoption rules before results; decisions use seed means of ≥ 3 training seeds and paired tests.
  • Deterministic physics: identical inputs give bitwise identical episodes (F68). Arena test splits are sealed and read once.
