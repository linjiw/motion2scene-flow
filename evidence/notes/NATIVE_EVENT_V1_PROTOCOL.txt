# Native relation event-sampling pilot v1

Written before candidate relabeling and fitting. Local exploratory registration,
not a blinded benchmark or external preregistration.

## Question

On the audited G1 reference geometry, does emphasizing near-boundary training
frames improve a fixed-budget body/obstacle relation reader relative to uniform
sampling or simply emphasizing the smallest clearance? Test this before adding
the reader to FM. This pilot trains a relation representation/readout, not a
navigation policy, a motion predictor, or a native SONIC token model.

## Data and labels

Use all 120 original clips and every original candidate obstacle (225 per clip),
including candidates excluded by the old clearance selector. Preserve source
ancestry and original splits: train has 100 clips in 11 groups (90 clips share
one group); development has 20 clips in 18 disjoint groups. Development was
previously used for audit, so call this a development comparison, not fresh test
generalization. No hyperparameter/arm/checkpoint selection using development.

Reuse the hash-bound audited USD shape/joint table and verify references and
candidate files against its receipts. Labels concern authored robot/obstacle
geometry; floor, self-collision, execution and cooked contact shapes remain out
of scope. Keep reference joint-limit violations visible; these poses may teach
geometry but are not valid motor imitation targets.

At each original source frame, record the minimum across all outer collision
shapes and the minimum across the actual primitive subset. A positive frame has
outer clearance > 0.02 m + 1e-8 m. A negative frame has primitive overlap < −1e-8 m.
Other frames are unknown and have no binary training loss. Mesh-sphere overlap
alone is never a collision witness. Negative proxy values are not penetration depths.

Whole-clip pair labels use the prior linear-XYZ/joints, shortest-arc-root-SLERP
interpolant. Any evaluated primitive overlap witnesses a negative. An interval
Lipschitz lower bound above the same margin certifies a positive. For pairs with
clear original frames but unresolved temporal bounds, bisect only unresolved
intervals, keeping already certified intervals; cap depth at 8 (256 subdivisions).
Never promote by dense sampling alone. Pairs already below the outer margin and
without a primitive witness remain unknown; do not spend refinement on them.
Unknown labels are retained in evaluation and never silently called negative.
Report refinement calls, remaining unknowns, and old 600-pair/240-scene retention.

## Learning comparison and information budget

Cross three samplers with two pooling methods and three seeds: 18 fits. All share
600 optimizer updates, batch 64, width 32, AdamW learning rate 0.001, final weights
only. Run on CPU with two threads, leaving the occupied GPU alone.

Each sampled training row chooses source group uniformly, then a clip uniformly,
then an obstacle with any labeled source frame uniformly. Choose positive/negative
class equally when both exist, otherwise use the available class. Share this
group/clip/obstacle/class schedule across all arms of the same seed. Only the
within-class source-frame selection changes:

- Uniform: uniform among eligible frames of the chosen class.
- Low clearance: 50% uniform, 50% uniform from the lowest outer-clearance quartile
  among those same eligible frames.
- Boundary: 50% uniform, 50% uniform among eligible positive frames within 0.02 m
  above the 0.02 m threshold or negative frames with primitive margin within
  0.02 m of zero. Fall back to uniform if that set is empty.

All labels and event scores are computed offline, for all methods. This tests a
training sampler at matched neural reads, not label-acquisition efficiency or a
deployable event detector. Report shared full-geometry preprocessing cost. No
clearance, interval label, candidate ID, split, ancestry, or class is a model input.

For each frame/obstacle the reader receives 45 shape tokens: two capsule endpoints
in obstacle coordinates, shape radius, obstacle half extents, sphere flag, and
primitive-subset flag (12 floats/token). A shared MLP embeds tokens; uniform or
learned attention pooling yields a 32-float representation and a binary head.
Nominal parameter counts match (uniform pooling disables learned attention scores).
This uses complete reference geometry, not partial robot observations.

Inference is identical for every learned arm: 16 uniformly spaced source frames,
45 body tokens/frame, whole-pair score = minimum predicted positive probability.
720 body-token reads/pair. A deterministic geometric comparator gets exactly these
same 16 frames; full continuous labels are the higher-cost evaluation reference.

## Outcomes and decisions

Primary: primitive-overlap witness rate among the top 30% scored development pairs,
reported with counts and unknown-accept rate. Acceptance is ranked over the entire
development pool with stable tie handling and floor(N×0.30). Unknowns cannot make
the witness rate a complete unsafe rate. Also report certified-positive acceptance,
conditional known-label balanced accuracy, and all pair-label counts.

Aggregate seeds without hiding any run. Compare boundary vs uniform within pooling
and attention vs uniform within sampler. Bootstrap the 18 development ancestry
groups, preserving paired predictions and averaging seed rates, for descriptive
95% intervals; keep fixed full-pool selection masks within resamples. Groups, clips,
candidate pairs, frames and fits are different units. No significance claim based
on thousands of correlated frames. No selected best seed/checkpoint.

Boundary support requires better witness rejection without replacing it by unknown
accepts; report tradeoffs and comparisons to low-clearance and geometric controls.
Mixed/null results constrain the next FM-conditioning design. Even a positive
result does not prove maximal information at tight maneuvers, learned execution,
goal-conditioned motion generation, compression of observations, or transfer to MTC.
